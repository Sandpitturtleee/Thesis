This thesis presents the implementation and analysis of classical and quantum minimum-finding methods used in
Dijkstra’s algorithm to solve the shortest-path problem. Three versions of the algorithm are analyzed: a naive version
using linear search, a classical version using a binary heap, and a quantum version based on Dürr and Høyer’s
minimum-finding algorithm, which utilizes the BBHT and Grover algorithms. The aim of the thesis is to investigate
the performance and correctness of the individual versions for graphs of different sizes and characteristics.
The study was conducted on sparse, medium-density, and dense graphs, as well as on a specially constructed graphs
designed to force the maximum number of relaxations. For each graph type and size, multiple graphs were generated,
followed by a statistical analysis including, the mean number of operations and standard deviation. In the case of
the quantum version, the probabilistic nature of the search and its impact on the correctness and variability of the
results were also taken into account. The obtained results show that the performance of the investigated
implementations depends to a large extent on the structure and density of the graph, its connectivity, edge weights,
and the course of the relaxation process. For the analyzed graphs, no performance advantage of the quantum
implementation over the classical solutions was observed.
